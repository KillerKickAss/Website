Notice Me! - How I aim to stand out in the crowded internet and blogspace

25 September, 2026

Online presence

It sounds obvious, but to be noticed online, you’ve got to be present, and you’ve got to be active online.

So how to be present online? Easy, have a blog and a website. Or better still, several blogs and several websites!

Social media

How to be active? As well as regularly posting new material for yourself, about yourself, checkout relevant posts on sites such as Facebook and LinkedIn. Comment on other people’s posts as well as posting yourself. Maybe post videos on YouTube or Instagram to show what you’re working on, such as part of a game with a commentary. And comment on other similar videos.

But I think the best platforms for Game Development are now Discord and Reddit.

Discord

Discord allows one “community” such as one for a game I’m working on, to have numerous channels associated with it.

I can have:

An Announcement Channel informing participants of general progress or difficulties

An Error or Bug Report Channel,

A Feature Request Channel,

A General Discussion Channel etc.

This is so that important information doesn’t get lost. And it enables me to have one to one conversations with participants. This builds loyalty. I let people know a regular time when I’ll be available for live interaction, and be available to answer questions etc.

I can get feedback from gamers and they can help influence and develop the game. And hopefully they’ll spread the word about the game to others.

Discord can also be used to create a closed community to allow initial and beta testing of a game.

Reddit

I think the other key resource to use is Reddit. Reddit’s organisation into subreddits enables targeting of specific groups. There are subreddits for different game genres. So I can post in areas where I know there’ll be gamers that enjoy the kind of game that I’m developing.

Reddit has a voting system that enables a popular post to get promoted so that many thousands of people see it. Well received posts stay visible for days or weeks.

It can be a very good way to get free marketing. But it is important to become actively involved in discussions, and share knowledge. Comment on others work, don’t just try to create an account to use it for free publicity for your own game.

Being relevant to employers

I also want to be noticed by potential employers, so that they will want me to work for them as a Game Developer!

To achieve this, I think it is important to know what skills an employer is looking for, and then for me to demonstrate that I’ve got those skills.

I think these are the key skills that I need to show:

  • I can code using programming languages such C++ (which seems to be the main the industry standard) as well as C#, Visual Studio, Java, DirectX and Python. I also understand data structures and algorithms.
  • I have reviewed code to ensure it is of good quality and can improve the design of code.
  • I can test code in a systematic way to find bugs and problems; and document where and what the problem was, and how I resolved it.
  • I have used 2D and 3D modelling software.
  • I have debugged programs for other people, and solved problems that occur in the development of a game
  • I have worked in a team to transform a game design into working code, sharing the work to produce an agreed outcome. Meeting regularly to achieve this and to ensure we each understand the objectives and overcome difficulties.
  • I have often had to work to tight deadlines and produced work on time.
  • I have undertaken all steps to produce a game, creating the initial concept, coding it, and problem solving in order to produce the end product of a working game.

I can put this information on my LinkedIn profile. But I think it is better to demonstrate these skills and experience by producing an online journal (in a blog). I can show the steps I’m taking when I’m producing a game. Day by day, step by step. What is the idea for the initial game design, and am I working alone or with a team. How I’m turning the design idea into code. What bugs and problems I find in the code and how I solve them. What other problems I’ve encountered. And the overall timeline from initial design to completed game.

Website

I’ll showcase my games on a website.

Whilst it is important for a website to look good, it is even more important that it functions correctly. And as more and more people only access websites on their mobile, it needs to look good and work well on a small mobile screen.

Most people (me included) aren’t professional web designers, so it is better to use a web template that others have produced. There are plenty of free templates to choose from, and then it is less likely the website will be over cluttered or with garish colours.

The main purpose of my website is to show off my games, and give some information about myself. It needs to include links to my blogs, LinkedIn, Discord, Reddit, and YouTube videos etc.

I only want to show my best work! I won’t put up all my work - so no games that are poorly designed or don’t work properly!

Trust Me! – Why I think it is so important to be professional

2 October 2026

What is Professionalism

Professionalism is a set of standards or guidelines that someone is expected to follow, usually in a workplace or in relation to delivering work. The standards are things like being punctual; being reliable; being organised; being responsible; being honest, having integrity and acting ethically; being polite and respectful or not being rude or abrupt, this is sometimes expressed as having emotional intelligence; and in some workplaces adhering to dress codes.

Pillars of Behaviour

Some people summarise Professionalism as being based on pillars of behaviour. The pillars most often quoted are:

Commitment – taking the work you’re doing seriously.

Integrity- acting morally and doing the right thing.

Responsibility – being accountable.

Respect – treating everyone fairly and appropriately.

Punctuality – arriving on time and completing work on time.

Initiative – acting independently, when appropriate, rather than just following instructions.

Why is Professionalism important in Game Development?

Game Development is complex and almost always involves many people to conceive, design, produce and market a game. The interaction between people and between different departments is key to successfully producing a game. When working collaboratively it is important to let everyone have input into the process – one person shouldn’t bulldoze their ideas through. After a discussion when a decision is reached, everyone needs to be on board with it, even if the outcome is not what I would have chosen.

Also working collaboratively, it is important for me and everyone to be punctual, and to meet agreed deadlines. If things I am doing go wrong, it is important for me to accept responsibility; and likewise for others to accept responsibility for their work.

Integrity in Game Development

There are a number of ways that a company or a freelancer producing games might not behave with integrity and ethically. Are staff being paid fairly, offered appropriate leave and training opportunities? Does the company have a fair recruitment process? Does the company (or freelancer) safeguard any personal information it may collect, and comply with GDPR. Does the company have equality and anti-bullying policies in place?

Also the content of the Game needs to be considered. Is the Game addictive? Does the Game put undue financial pressure on gamers to purchase extras? Does the Game glorify violence? Does the Game stereotype Heroes and Villains, and is it being (unwittingly) racist, misogynistic, homophobic etc - Is it culturally sensitive?

The Company or Developer needs to consider their environmental impact. They should consider what equipment is used, choosing energy efficient hardware. Also they need to consider the supply chain for any merchandise offered. Are there fair labour practices in place for suppliers?

Not only is it the right thing to do, if a company or freelancer is seen to be acting with integrity and ethically, they are more likely to develop long term loyalty and a positive relationship with players.

Professional codes of conduct

There is no universally accepted Code of Conduct and Ethical Behaviour for Game Development, but two organisations IGDA and W3C have developed Ethical Codes and Guidelines. The International Game Developers Association (IGDA)

This is the largest not for profit organisation for Game Developers. IGDA recognises the importance of the effect of ideas conveyed through art, and ideas presented in an interactive choice-driven format, such as a video game.

The Code of Ethics has the following objectives:

  • To promote the growth of the gaming industry;
  • To cultivate a supportive community of game developers;
  • To speak on behalf of game developers to ensure their views are understood;
  • To emphasise empathy in offering diverse perspectives and experiences;
  • To encourage inclusivity for developers, studios, and games.

The Inclusivity Policy aims to support equality in the workplace, cordial respectful communications, safe social interactions, and an environment that reflects fairness and mutual respect throughout the industry.

You can see the full code here

The World Wide Web Consortium (W3C).

The aim of the W3C Ethical Guidelines is that the web should empower an equitable, informed, and interconnected society.

The Principles are that:

  • There is one web .
  • The web does not cause harm to society
  • The web supports healthy community and debate
  • The web is for all people
  • The web is secure and respects people's privacy
  • The web enables freedom of expression
  • The web makes it possible to verify information
  • The web enhances individuals' control and power
  • The web is an environmentally sustainable platform
  • The web is transparent
  • The web is multi-browser, multi-OS, and multi-device
  • The web can be consumed in any way that people choose

You can see the full W3C Ethical Web Principles here: https://www.w3.org/TR/ethical-web-principles/

These two codes can help Game Developers work ethically.

Showing Respect

I’ve outlined how a Game Developer should show respect to other staff, clients and an employer. Failure to act respectfully and professionally can seriously damage your reputation. If you have a poor reputation, very quickly employers will stop wanting to employ you, and clients won’t use you.

Pay Me! – Surviving as an Indie Game Developer

9 October 2026

What is a Freelancer?

Being freelance essentially means that I’m self-employed with all the pros and cons that go with that. I sell my skills to a number of different clients rather than just one employer.

Skills

To be successful as an Indy Game Developer (i.e a freelancer) I’ll need skills to run as a business, not just game development skills.

I’ll have to be able to:

  • Manage budgets and choose the right price to charge for my work. I need to choose carefully as no client wants escalating costs. If there is uncertainty in timescales or complexity, I have to be clear about this at the start, outlining anything that may change.
  • Time Manage and Meet Deadlines. Once agreed I must stick to deadlines even if something more exciting or more lucrative comes up. My professional integrity means that I have keep to agreements and meet deadlines.
  • Communicate Clearly. I must be able to both talk and write to clients and any team members I’m working with setting out expectations on roles, budgets, timelines and contingencies.
  • Manage contracts and invoices. I’ll use a proforma contract adapted for each job. I’ll have to a process and invoices clients at agreed intervals.
  • I’ll have to plan my own professional development. Game development is changing all the time so I must continue to develop my skills. I must find, pay for and attend courses or events that will help me develop.
  • Know about general legislation such as Health and Safety, and GDPR.

How to Promote Myself.

In freelance work I must be able to demonstrate what I can do. I’ll have:

  • A good and up to date CV and portfolio of previous work I have done so I can show a client examples of what I’ve previously done and delivered. It will include problems encountered and solved as well as the finished game.
  • A statement about my professional standards and integrity.
  • A portfolio website. This will show in one place, who I am, what I can do and how to contact me.
  • A social media presence.
  • Links to groups or networks that promote game development and attend relevant events I’ll use sites that help find clients

As a freelancer there can be a tendency to keep working, and not take holidays or rest enough!!!

The Importance of Reputation

Reputation in a professional sense is basically what others’ opinion of me are. It positively or negatively influences what work a freelancer can get and so requires consistent thought and effort.

It includes:

  • Being Reliable. Doing what I say I will do on time.
  • Giving credit to others. Game development is built on team working, so I’ll build positive relationships with others, and try to be supportive.
  • Acting with integrity. Being honest and trustworthy, delivering the best results I can. This also means, for example, not leaking a trailer before it comes out, not breaking a non- disclosure agreement or not complaining about a studio.
  • Developing my skills and knowledge
  • Keeping communication constructive, respectful and discrete. Game development can be stressful when working to tight deadlines which can lead to disagreements. I won’t engage in gossip or rumour, but try to stay calm and focused on the issue in hand.
  • Communicating if things go wrong and taking responsibility; focusing on solutions.

Benefits and Drawbacks of Freelancing

Benefits

  • Creative Independence.
  • Not being tied to the micromanagement of big studios.
  • Games take some time to develop and I won’t have to be tied to a long term project.
  • The game will be just mine and the team I’ve worked with so I’ll be credited at the end, which may not happen in a big company.
  • I’ll get to choose which genre I want and can experiment with different ways of doing things. It may be my intellectual property.
  • Flexibility and Variety. As long as I meet deadlines I’ll be able to choose when and where to work, and can choose work with different game engines.
  • I’ll have the potential to earn more, perhaps.

Drawbacks.

  • No guaranteed income. This is the biggest drawback, and may be stressful!
  • No employer benefits. No holiday pay, sick pay or pension. I’ll have to deal with the bureaucracy of employment such as tax. I’ll also have to provide my own equipment and comply with things like GDPR.
  • Unexpected costs like computer breakdown, or software licences.
  • Isolation. It may be isolating working for myself, alone.

The Appeal of Freelancing and Indie Development.

The idea of making Indie games has appealed to me since I was a child and I am doing my best to learn the skills that will help me do this. The flexibility of the working hours would suit me and I can work from home without a problem. My living costs are low, so I am lucky enough to be able to go through periods of time with little income.

The main reason is so that I could get to pick the games that I am interested in, and I would like to follow through my ideas to completion. I feel passionate about games and would love to see people play them and enjoy them.

I have had mental health issues in the past and would not respond well to a very disciplined crunch culture. This would increase my anxiety.

For me developing games is fun and I want to share my ideas and make a career from them.

AAA Game Development versus Indie Games.

What is AAA Game Development.

These are games developed by big studios, with huge budgets and huge multidisciplinary teams of around 300+.

  • Bigger teams can lead to hierarchy and bureaucracy which can be good and bad.
  • Teams have opportunity for specialism
  • They have professional actors and orchestral standard in music.
  • They are large scale projects and have a world audience.
  • They require big investment and bring in as much if not more than Hollywood blockbusters making billions for their platforms. GTA is an example of this.
  • It pushes technology to its limits and every part of it is fine tuned. They have access to advanced technologies like smart AI and real time ray tracing.
  • Their stories are complex as much as any feature film.
  • They are influential in how future games will be developed.
  • Their environments are realistic and immersive.
  • There are huge budgets to market them.

What is Indie Game development.

  • Much smaller budgets that often rely on small loans or kickstarter funding.
  • Platforms like Patreon allow subscribers to fund the game allowing them to feel invested in the process.
  • Small teams with as little as one developer doing everything.
  • Indie games are distributed digitally rather than through retail
  • Can be more experimental in their creativity as they are not appealing to a mass market.
  • Working in small teams can allow everyone to express their creativity.
  • Indie developers rely much more on the creativity of their team to make up for the lack of resources. This means there is less room for things to go wrong.
  • They can have an all hand on deck attitude as there are less specialists to rely on.
  • They can be much less bureaucratic jumping into the game without the documentation that big teams require.

Establishing my own business.

Getting organised.

This will be a business so I’ll need business space. I’ll use a dedicated space in the house.. There is no office cleaner so I’ll have to keep it tidy! I’ll use a digital calendar to map out projects, dates and deadlines. Toggi and Trello can help keep track of the hours I’ll work.

Finding potential clients.

My network is important here, and I’ll search on LinkedIn and Instagram. I’ll reach out to contacts when there I don’t have much work.

Having an up to date portfolio.

Clients will want to know what I’ve done, so it’s a good idea to have it all in one place. My portfolio will show the work I’m most proud of and displays a variety of my skills to show what I’m capable of. The end page will include all my contact details including my social media accounts. I’ll include what inspires me and where I get ideas from.

Embrace your own self-worth.

I’ll be enthusiastic about my skills. For many, like me, this is difficult, but if I hide away, nobody’s going to find me. I may invest in a marketing plan.

Know your legal rights.

I will have to have contracts, so some investment in making good ones is worthwhile, including payment terms and copyright.

Finance.

I’ll work out how much I need to earn to meet my requirements. I’ll set your rates and be prepared to negotiate. Chase invoices. I’ll register as self-employed to pay tax and keep accounts.

Mental Health.

Working on your own can be lonely. It can be difficult to turn down work and end up working all the time which can lead to burn out. Enjoy downtime.

General Advice.

There are pros and cons to all sectors in game development. I recommend anyone thinks carefully about whether you can manage the downside in what you think you prefer. In freelance, are you prepared to be a small business with all that this entails, including insecure income, investing in administration and paying for equipment and services? Will you be able to cope with the loneliness and responsibility? Does the freedom to pursue your own creativity make up for this?

In working for a small Indie company are you prepared to do a bit of everything rather than develop and expertise in your chosen field. Can you live with the financial uncertainty and can you afford to invest in equipment.

If you are thinking of working for a Triple A platform, will the financial benefits and greater job security make up for the defined roles and structures of working in a large organisation?

Written by Me! – All my own work. Why Intellectual Property Rights matter

23 October 2026

What is IP

IP (Intellectual property) generally refers to a unique creation from the mind. Examples are works of art, music, literature and computer programs.

The importance of IP is established in various laws which protect the IP Rights. Laws on IP are local rights, so differ from country to country. Also there are different laws for different kinds of IP. Generally laws prevent someone from taking or copying the protected IP without permission and usually any permission granted will be limited and require payment.

The protection of IP Rights is important for humanity. Without it there would be little incentive for anyone to come up with new ideas that take a long time to develop, if anyone could just walk off with the resulting work without permission and without any recompense. Scientific and technical innovation would be seveerly hindered.

With IP Rights inventors, artists, scientists and businesses can put time, money, energy and thought into developing new ideas and creations knowing they can benefit financially from their work. IP Rights give the creator control over how their work (their IP) is used.

There are a number of ways that IP is protected, depending on exactly what the IP is. Protection can come through Patents, Copyright and Trademarks.

Computer software, art and music are protected by Copyright Law, so that is the most important way that Computer Games are protected.

IP Rights in creative industries and Game Development.

As I’ve discussed previously, Computer Games are usually huge pieces of work. Producing a Game can involve many people, programming software, with bespoke art and specially commissioned music. The investment in time and money can be huge. Without a method to monetise the final product, Computer Games would never be produced. The Game Developer owns the IP Rights to the Game. Copyright Law protects the IP Rights and enables the IP Rights owner to charge players money to use the Game and so recoup the cost (and hopefully make a profit). It stops anyone from just copying the game and giving it away for free.

Copyright Protection

Copyright laws have largely been successful up to now to protect IP Rights for their owners, so that fair compensation can be charged for the work that the owners have done. But AI now threatens this.

Generative AI models are trained with vast amounts of data from the internet, including copyrighted material. The large AI companies generally do not pay anything for “scraping” this data. Even if they were to pay, what would a fair payment be?

Take the example of music produced and issued by a musician, say Adele. If the AI is trained with all the music from Adele, it can then “learn” to imitate her. Any subscriber to the AI could ask for it to play music in the style of Adele. Consequently many people could enjoy Adele style music in future without having to pay fees for listening to her music. Her income from her body of work could dramatically decline. And any new music she produced is likely to be far less successful as it would have been.

What would a fair payment be? The same fee as a human would pay to listen once to all her past work, or a fee equivalent to the value of all her work? And that assumes there would even be a mechanism to collect payment from the foreign AI companies.

Games are similarly at risk. Eventually AI will be able produce a Game, say, in the style of Grand Theft Auto at a tiny fraction of the cost.

Legal Challenges

Whilst Copyright protects against copying software or music, it is not clear if it would also protect against AI producing something that is similar rather than the same.

In 2024 Getty Images brought a case against Stability AI Limited claiming copyright infringement by copying and using 12 million images to train the AI. After numerous hearings, Getty Images dropped the claim related to using the images but proceeded with a claim that some of the outputted images from the AI (from the training) was so similar to some of the originals including a Getty trademark that it represented copyright infringement. The case was eventually heard in the High Court in London this year and Getty lost. So there is no legal case at present in the UK stopping AI from using copyright material.

Who Benefits from AI generated Art for Games?

AI art is faster and cheaper to produce, so in the short term it may slightly reduce the cost of Games. It is also helpful for solo Game Designers who may now have access to better Art than just accessing stock pictures.

The obvious winners are the AI companies, and who may start charging more for access to the AI. The obvious losers are the entry level artists who will no longer be needed. Some human artists will still be required to refine the AI output, but the number of Art jobs will definitely diminish.

Players may also suffer as AI Art which is all trained on similar data will start producing more similar output. There will be less variety. AI Art is only as good as the brief it is given, and probably won’t be able to reproduce Art with the appropriate emotional resonance.

In my view, AI is undoubtedly a threat to the Creative Industries.
